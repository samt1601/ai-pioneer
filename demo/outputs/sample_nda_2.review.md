# Review: sample_nda_2.txt

**Overall: 🔴 RED**

Titled mutual but drafted one-way, with eleven escalation triggers including a residuals clause, a non-compete dressed as a non-solicit, feedback assignment, perpetual obligations and liquidated damages. This is not a fast-lane document. Route to senior counsel; the realistic options are negotiating onto our paper or a substantial rewrite.

| Clause | Ref | Status | Finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Confidential Information | `definition` | 🔴 | Catch-all definition covering anything disclosed in any form, with no marking requirement and no reasonableness qualifier. Also note the definition only covers information disclosed BY Veltrix, which is the mutuality problem in miniature. |
| 2. Obligations of Company | `mutuality` | 🔴 | All substantive obligations fall on Company only, under a document titled mutual. Veltrix takes on no confidentiality obligations anywhere in the agreement. |
| 3. Exclusions | `carveouts` | 🔴 | Only two of the five standard carve-outs are present. Missing: independent development, receipt from a third party, and disclosure required by law. The prior-knowledge carve-out is also narrowed by a written-records evidentiary burden. |
| 4. Residual Knowledge | `residuals` | 🔴 | One-way residuals clause. Per playbook these gut the agreement and always escalate; this one runs in only one direction on top. |
| 5. Feedback | `ip` | 🔴 | Outright assignment of feedback and ownership of all derivatives. Playbook treats any IP assignment as an escalation. A narrow-licence counter exists in precedent if counsel chooses to negotiate rather than strike. |
| 6. Non-Solicitation and Business Protection | `non_compete` | 🔴 | A non-compete drafted inside a clause labelled non-solicitation, covering anything Veltrix even contemplated, for 24 months. Playbook: never acceptable in an NDA, however phrased. |
| 6. Non-Solicitation and Business Protection | `non_solicit` | 🔴 | One-way, 24 months, all employees, no general-advertising carve-out. Exceeds every limit in the acceptable fallback. Precedent counter is mutual, six months, material-contact scope. |
| 7. Term | `term` | 🔴 | Indefinite term terminable only by the counterparty. Effectively perpetual at Veltrix's option. |
| 7. Term | `confidentiality_survival` | 🔴 | Indefinite survival applied to all confidential information, with no narrowing to trade secrets. Direct escalation trigger. |
| 8. Remedies | `remedies` | 🔴 | Liquidated damages plus a one-way indemnity including attorneys' fees. Precedent: senior counsel previously renegotiated an identical $50k clause to injunctive relief plus actual damages; treat as red, no exceptions. |
| 9. Assignment | `assignment` | 🔴 | Asymmetric: counterparty assigns freely, we cannot. Matches the playbook escalation trigger exactly. |
| 10. Governing Law and Jurisdiction | `governing_law` | 🔴 | Exclusive foreign jurisdiction combined with both a jury-trial waiver and fee-shifting: the precise escalation trigger. Precedent counter to England and Wales exists if negotiation proceeds. |
## Escalations

- Mutuality: one-way obligations under a mutual title (clause 2)
- Definition: catch-all with no marking or reasonableness qualifier (clause 1)
- Carve-outs: three of five standard exclusions missing (clause 3)
- Residuals clause present and one-way (clause 4)
- IP: outright assignment of feedback and derivatives (clause 5)
- Non-compete embedded in non-solicit clause (clause 6(b))
- Non-solicit: one-way, 24 months, all employees (clause 6(a))
- Term and survival: perpetual at counterparty's option (clause 7)
- Remedies: liquidated damages and one-way indemnity (clause 8)
- Assignment: asymmetric (clause 9)
- Jurisdiction: exclusive NY courts with jury waiver and fee-shifting (clause 10)
